SECTION 01

Commander's Intent

The intent states the purpose, the essential conditions that must be created, and the acceptable shape of the force at the end of Day 1. It remains authoritative when the detailed plan no longer fits the situation.

Purpose
Preserve coalition freedom of action in the Philippine approaches, protect the force and the resupply mission, and impose a reversible operational dilemma on RED without widening the conflict.
Key tasks
  • Maintain a continuous, resilient recognized maritime and air picture.
  • Protect TG B-1, TG B-3, Palawan, and the Philippine resupply corridor.
  • Locate and hold RED’s principal naval striking forces at risk.
  • Deny RED a favorable first salvo through dispersal, deception, CAP, and ASW.
  • Preserve scarce long-range weapons, tankers, AEW, and submarines for decisive windows.
Desired end state · 2400Z

The resupply force remains viable; BLUE’s carrier and amphibious forces retain maneuver room east of the archipelago; RED has not achieved local sea control or a coercive fait accompli; BLUE maintains credible strike and undersea capacity; escalation boundaries remain intact unless deliberately released; and the force is postured to continue, disengage, or escalate on order.

Risk accepted / risk refused

Accept: temporary gaps in broad-area surveillance and limited tactical disadvantage to preserve high-value enablers. Refuse: an unprotected concentration of high-value units, uncontrolled weapons release, loss of the resupply force through inattention, or exposing submarines merely to improve the common picture.

MAIN EFFORTSea denial west of Palawan

Protect the resupply corridor and prevent RED surface or submarine forces from establishing a blocking position.

SUPPORTING EFFORT 01Fleet preservation east of Luzon

Keep TG B-1 combat credible under disciplined EMCON while building the contact picture required for later offensive action.

SUPPORTING EFFORT 02Counter-reconnaissance

Disrupt RED’s kill chain with deception, selective emissions, fighter interception, and movement—without trading scarce aircraft for low-value contacts.

SECTION 02

Navalized METT-TC

M-METT-TC translates the familiar estimate into maritime terms: forces move in three dimensions, information is intermittent, magazines are finite, and reach depends on bases, tankers, sensors, and political access.

MMission

Protect, reveal, and preserve options

During Day 1, maintain coalition access to the western Philippine approaches, shield the Palawan resupply effort, and prevent RED from converting peacetime presence into local sea control. Gain enough contact quality to hold hostile strike forces at risk while keeping offensive action bounded by political release.

Essential task: the convoy and covering group survive with a viable onward route; the force is not required to destroy the RED fleet on Day 1.
EEnemy

Layered reconnaissance-strike complex

RED combines land-based fighters and bombers, island and mainland sensors, a carrier group, central-basin SAG, amphibious group, diesel submarines near chokepoints, and SSNs on wider approaches. Its likely preference is to localize BLUE, compress reaction time, and present an ambiguous coercive action under dense air-defense cover.

Most dangerous: coordinated missile attack enabled by persistent multi-axis ISR while a submarine exploits force displacement.
MMaritime environment

Archipelagic, cluttered, sensor-dependent

Luzon, Palawan, the Spratlys, and the Paracels divide the battlespace into constrained approaches. Islands extend radar and SAM coverage; coastal clutter complicates classification; deep water favors nuclear submarines while shelves and straits can favor quiet diesel boats. Weather and sea state may close sensor windows without closing weapon envelopes.

TTroops / forces

Capable, distributed, enabler-limited

BLUE brings TG B-1, the Philippine SAG, TG B-3, four SSNs, 46 land-based fighters, P-8/MQ-9 coverage, AEW, tankers, coastal-defense forces, and geographically useful bases. The force’s practical limits are sortie generation, tanker/AEW survivability, long-range weapon inventories, uncertain undersea contacts, and command coherence across dispersed units.

TTime

Twenty-four hours; four decision windows

The first six hours favor posture, search, and deception. Hours 06–12 should improve classification and expose RED intent. Hours 12–18 are the likely decisive commitment window. Hours 18–24 must consolidate, re-arm, rescue, and establish the next day’s geometry. Aircraft cycle in minutes; submarines and surface formations shape tomorrow’s fight over hours.

CCivil considerations

Legitimacy is operational freedom

Neutral states, civilian air and maritime traffic, Philippine sovereignty, and the visibility of encounters constrain identification and weapons release. Mainland China, Guam, and civilian infrastructure are protected from attack absent explicit escalation. Every ambiguous contact demands disciplined classification; every engagement will influence coalition access and political endurance.

Default: positive identification, proportional response, and immediate reporting of actions near neutral or civilian traffic.
SECTION 03

Navalized OCOKA

M-OCOKA treats geography, spectrum, water column, and basing as one maneuver environment. Its question is not simply “where can forces go?” but “where can they detect, classify, engage, hide, and sustain?”

O
Observation & engagement geometrySensor-to-shooter fields
Airborne early warning, space-based cueing, island radars, P-8 patrols, carrier aviation, and surface sensors create overlapping but fragile detection zones. Radar horizon, emission control, contact latency, and identification quality matter more than nominal sensor range.
COMMAND USEKeep at least two dissimilar sensing paths on each priority axis. Do not treat a stale contact as targeting-quality data.
C
Cover & concealmentEMCON · clutter · depth
The Philippine landmass masks some low-altitude movement; commercial traffic and littoral clutter complicate classification; weather can conceal or blind; the water column and ambient noise offer undersea concealment. EMCON reduces signature but also degrades coordination and local awareness.
COMMAND USEUse selective emissions, off-axis datalinks, decoys, and movement. Expose a sensor only for a defined information gain.
O
Obstacles & constraintsPhysical · threat · political
Luzon and Palawan passages, reef systems, shallow shelves, RED SAM/anti-ship envelopes, tanker dependence, neutral traffic, and restricted strike areas all channel action. An obstacle may stop one domain while leaving another open; a political boundary can be as decisive as a reef or missile battery.
COMMAND USEPlot threat and policy boundaries together. Preserve an exit route and an alternate recovery base for every committed package.
K
Key maritime terrainControl disproportionate effect
The Palawan resupply corridor, western Luzon air approaches, Luzon Strait, central-basin maneuver space, and the airfields at Basa, Antonio Bautista, and Mactan shape reach and endurance. Fiery Cross and Woody Island act as sensor, air-defense, and sortie-generation nodes rather than mere land features.
COMMAND USEPrioritize function over possession: deny surveillance, launch capacity, or passage at the time it matters.
A
Avenues of approachSurface · air · subsurface
Primary surface axes run from Hainan through the Paracels into the central basin and from the Spratlys toward Palawan. Air avenues converge from Hainan, the reefs, and carrier launch areas. Subsurface approaches exploit the western Luzon barrier, Palawan passages, Spratly chokepoints, and deeper Philippine Sea routes.
COMMAND USEBuild cross-domain barriers, not single lines. Force RED to reveal or reroute, then shift the mobile reserve against the exposed axis.

SECTION 05

Control & Assessment

A small set of decision points and measures keeps execution aligned to intent without over-controlling dispersed commanders.

Commander's critical information

PIR 01
Indications that RED is preparing or initiating a coordinated maritime strike.
PIR 02
A submarine contact with a credible path to TG B-1, TG B-3, or the resupply corridor.
PIR 03
RED surface or amphibious movement that can create a fait accompli within 12 hours.
FFIR 01
Loss or mission kill of an AEW, tanker, carrier/amphibious ship, or key runway.
FFIR 02
Defensive magazine or sortie-generation capacity falling below the ability to defeat one more raid.

Authorities & boundaries

Self-defense
Always retained consistent with scenario ROE and positive identification requirements.
Mainland
Strikes not authorized without explicit escalation event and command release.
Guam
Protected from attack under the initial scenario state.
Civilian
No deliberate effects against civilian infrastructure; maintain proportionality and track separation.
Submarines
Do not compromise covert disposition for routine reporting or low-value prosecution.
